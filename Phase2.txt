Phase 2 — UI/UX Architecture
We’ll now design the actual application experience. The key rule from Phase 1 carries forward:
Athlete and Coach are two different products inside the same platform.
They should share the design system and backend, but their navigation, priorities, dashboards, and workflows should be distinctly different.

2.1 Overall App Structure
After authentication:
                         LOGIN
                           │
                           ▼
                     ROLE ROUTER
                           │

              ▼                         ▼
         ATHLETE APP               COACH APP
              │                         │
       ┌──────┼──────┐           ┌──────┼──────┐
       ▼      ▼      ▼           ▼      ▼      ▼
    Home   Workout Progress    Home  Athletes Programs
We should not have one dashboard that changes a few labels depending on the role.

2.2 Design Philosophy
The Athlete UI should answer:
"What should I do today?"
The Coach UI should answer:
"Who needs my attention, and what should I do about them?"
That gives us two different information architectures.
Athlete
Action → Progress → Motivation → Guidance
Coach
Overview → Problems → Athletes → Programs → Analysis

Phase 2F — AI Trainer
This is the layer that can turn the app from a gym tracking app into an intelligent training platform.
The key architectural principle:
The AI doesn't own the training system. It reads structured training data, reasons over it, and proposes actions. The platform decides what can actually be changed.
That distinction will matter enormously once we have real users.

20. AI Safety Architecture
The AI should have strict boundaries.
                AI
                 │
         ┌───────┴────────┐
         ▼                ▼
    Fitness Guidance   Medical Boundary
         │                │
         ▼                ▼
 Training data       Don't diagnose
 recommendations     Don't prescribe
If the user reports something that sounds like an injury or medical issue, the app should not pretend to diagnose it.
For example:
"My shoulder hurts when I bench."
The AI should not confidently say:
"You have rotator cuff tendinitis."
Instead it can recommend stopping/adjusting the aggravating activity and seeking appropriate professional evaluation when warranted.

33. Athlete Detail API
Rather than making the app load 20 endpoints:
GET /api/v1/coaches/me/athletes/:athleteId
returns the overview.
Then detailed screens request specific data:
GET /athletes/:id/workouts
GET /athletes/:id/progress
GET /athletes/:id/goals
GET /athletes/:id/program
This keeps large responses manageable.
