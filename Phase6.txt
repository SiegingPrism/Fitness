Yes. At this point, improving the existing product and expanding the actual fitness content is the right move. You have enough infrastructure. Continuing to build infrastructure before improving the product would be premature.
What I would not do now is randomly add 50 exercises and 20 screens. We should move into a dedicated Product Enhancement Phase where we systematically improve what users already experience.

6A Should Be Our Immediate Start
I recommend we start by massively improving the exercise/workout database, because it becomes the foundation for almost everything else.
Our first expansion should target roughly:
Chest          25–35
Back           30–40
Shoulders      20–25
Biceps         15–20
Triceps        15–20
Quads          20–25
Hamstrings     15–20
Glutes         15–20
Calves         10–15
Core           20–25
Full Body      15–20
Conditioning   15–20
Mobility       15–20
That gives us roughly 250–300 well-structured exercises, rather than hundreds of poorly defined duplicates.
Then we'll build the exercise alternatives, search/filtering, exercise detail pages, workout templates, supersets, dropsets, RPE/RIR, rest timers, progression logic, and better workout completion experience around that database.
So yes: stop expanding infrastructure for now. We should enter a product-building cycle. The next concrete step is Phase 6A: redesign the Exercise schema + build the master exercise catalog, then populate it systematically.
